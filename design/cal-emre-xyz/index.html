A small mark for a calendar that belongs to you

The site is quiet, near-black on warm white, with one green dot to say "this day is open". The proposal keeps that voice. The logo is that dot, given a home.

Three logo directions

All three are a rounded tile, so they drop straight into the current header and work as a favicon. Each is shown large, then at 32, 24 and 16 px.

recommended

A · Open slot

A grid of time slots with one lit in green. It is what visitors see on the booking page, so the brand and the product say the same thing. It stays readable at 16 px because the lit dot is the only bright element.

safest

B · Pinned day

Your current calendar icon with the green dot added. Nobody has to relearn it. It is the least distinctive of the three, since any calendar app uses this shape.

boldest

C · Monogram

A "c" that leaves its mouth open, with the dot sitting in the gap as a booked slot. It reads as a letter first and a calendar second, and it holds up best at tiny sizes.

Wordmark and lockups

Shown with concept A. The dot in the domain is green, so the name carries the same signal as the mark. "NIP-52" stays as the small mono line you already have.

Published as NIP-52 events on open relays. One line, link to nostr.org.tr.
  • Duration as four chips15, 30, 45, 60 cover most meetings. "Longer" opens the rest. Nine equal buttons make the common choice harder to see.
  • Show only open timesFold past and booked slots into one line. The list then shows what the visitor can actually pick.
  • Form replaces the slot listAfter a time is chosen the form opens in that same column, so the calendar stays in view and the choice can be changed with one click.
  • Timezone near the timesKeep the visitor's timezone selector directly above the slots, defaulting to the browser's zone, not Atlantic/Reykjavik.
